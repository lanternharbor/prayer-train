# Outreach templates: men's parish organizations

Drafts William can adapt and send from his personal email when reaching
out to men's-side parish leadership. Tone is intentionally warm,
peer-level, names the context, and asks for nothing — same shape as
the cold outreach he sent to Kathleen Lubowski about the Spina train.

The Catholic intercessory-prayer infrastructure (parish prayer chains,
novena groups, perpetual help societies) skews roughly 2:1 female by
volunteer rate. PrayerTrain has inherited that baseline so far —
every non-Keough-organized train has routed through a women's parish
ministry. The brand identity is gender-balanced (locomotive imagery,
"aunties and uncles" / "grandmas and papas" copy on /our-story), but
the distribution funnel hasn't been pointed at men's-side leadership.
These templates are the first move on that.

The lead variant is for Knights of Columbus (most numerous, most
parishes have a council). Adaptation notes for other men's orgs at
the bottom.

---

## Lead variant: Knights of Columbus

**Subject**: a Catholic prayer-coordination tool for your council

```
Hi [Grand Knight / Name],

I'm William Keough, a lay parishioner from the South Shore. I built a
tool called PrayerTrain (prayertrains.com) that organizes intercessory
prayer the way a meal train organizes meals: a shared calendar, friends
and family signing up for one prayer slot at a time, and a printable
spiritual bouquet for the family at the end.

It's free, no accounts required to claim a slot, and built around
Catholic devotion (rosaries, novenas, chaplets, Mass intentions). The
first non-family-organized train rallied 38 prayer warriors in 48 hours
through a women's parish ministry. I think it would land just as well
through a Knights of Columbus council. The kind of practical,
hands-on service work the Knights are built for.

I'm not asking for anything. I wanted you to know it exists, in case
it's useful when a brother knight or a parishioner is walking through
something hard. There's a one-page handout I can send if it would
help, meant for the back of a parish after Mass or to hand out at a
council meeting.

Pax,
William
william@lanternharbor.co
```

## Adaptation notes by organization

### ACTS (Adoration, Community, Theology, Service)

- Swap **council** → **team**
- Swap **Grand Knight** → **Director** (or the team lead's actual title)
- Swap **Knights of Columbus** → **ACTS team**
- Swap **brother knight** → **team brother**
- The "practical, hands-on service work" line still fits ACTS culture
  (service is one of the four pillars).

### Holy Name Society

- Swap **council** → **society chapter**
- Swap **Grand Knight** → **President**
- Swap **Knights of Columbus** → **Holy Name Society**
- Swap **brother knight** → **fellow Holy Name member**
- The "practical, hands-on service work" line is weaker here (Holy
  Name is more devotional than service-oriented). Consider replacing
  with: "the kind of devotional commitment your society is built
  around."

### That Man Is You / men's faith-formation groups

- Swap **council** → **chapter** or **group**
- Swap **Grand Knight** → leader's first name
- Swap **Knights of Columbus** → group name
- Swap **brother knight** → **brother**
- Drop the "practical, hands-on service work" line entirely; replace
  with something fit to the group's character (e.g., for TMIY: "the
  kind of brotherhood you've already built around the Saturday
  morning sessions").

### Generic men's group / parish men's ministry

- Swap to **men's group** and the leader's first name
- Drop the gendered framing on the "first non-family-organized train"
  sentence if the audience is mixed-gender; substitute: "rallied 38
  prayer warriors in 48 hours through a parish ministry."

### Mixed-gender parish leadership (pastor, parish council, deacon)

- Drop the explicit men's-org framing entirely
- Lead with: "I'm reaching out because your parish hosts the kind of
  community that already organizes around its members."
- The rest of the email reads cleanly without changes.

## How to use this file

1. Copy the lead variant (Knights of Columbus) or the closest
   adaptation.
2. Edit the subject and salutation for the specific recipient.
3. Verify no em dashes survived the edit pass (preference: commas,
   colons, periods, semicolons instead).
4. Send from william@lanternharbor.co (the address that signs the
   parish handout, so the lineage is consistent if a recipient
   already has a copy of the handout).
5. If the recipient asks for the handout, send `printables/parish-handout.pdf`
   and `printables/parish-cards.pdf` as attachments.

## What not to include

- No "we got through" / "all three children came out on top" framing.
  Same reasoning as the parish-handout copy: many parish men have a
  loved one walking through a serious diagnosis, and the audience
  shouldn't have to read a triumph narrative.
- No PrayerTrain feature deep-dives. The recipient cares about
  whether this would help his brother knights / society members; he
  doesn't need to learn the data model.
- No mention of the Spina family by name. The "first non-family-
  organized train" detail is enough to ground the social proof
  without naming a grieving family.
